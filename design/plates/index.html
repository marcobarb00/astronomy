Three blue plates for Act I

Each one where it would go, before deciding

A blue plate is a static chart of the sky in the style of a cyanotype star atlas (STYLE.md §7). Below, each proposed plate is shown after the paragraph of its page that it would follow, quoted in grey. The pages themselves are unchanged.

Page 1.1 · ObservationThe sky around the pole

Where: after the paragraph on latitude, before the present Figure 1 (the observer's sky). What it adds: the real stars around the pole, with Ptolemy's circle of stars that never set at Rome. Cost: Figures 1 to 4 become 2 to 5, so the page's own references to them change number.

The part of the sky that can be seen therefore depends on the latitude. Travelling north, "the more of the southern stars disappear and the more of the northern stars appear" (Almagest I 4). Canopus, the second brightest star, can be seen from Rhodes but never from Rome.
