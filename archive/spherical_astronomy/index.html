The celestial sphere

Why a sphere. Stars are so far away that, seen from Earth, they all seem to sit on one huge sphere centered on us. We only use directions, so only angles on this sphere matter.

Why the observer does not matter. Earth is a point compared to the sphere, so two observers at different places see a star at the same angular position.

Motion 2 · intrinsic

The drift. On top of the diurnal rotation, the sun, moon and planets slowly drift west→east against the stars.

The ecliptic. The sun traces one fixed great circle in a year, the ecliptic. It is tilted by ε = 23°26′ to the celestial equator. Its position on the ecliptic is its ecliptic longitude λ☉ (move the Sun λ☉ slider). The sun's declination follows from the tilt:

At λ☉ = 90° this gives δ☉ = ε, the summer solstice.

The vernal equinox V

Definition. The two circles cross at two points. V is the one where the sun crosses the equator going north, called the vernal or spring equinox. The other is the autumnal equinox V′, directly opposite.

V is not stationary with respect to the fixed stars. Search precession of equinoxes to learn more.
